Homechevron_rightToolschevron_rightKorean Salary Calculator

A Korean salary is quoted as a headline number, and the amount that reaches your account is a good deal smaller. Four insurance contributions and two taxes come off it first. Put the offer in, say how many people are in your household, and this works out what is actually left — plus what the salaries either side of yours are worth, which is the comparison that matters when you are weighing an offer or asking for a rise.

list_alt How to use Korean Salary Calculator

  1. Choose whether you are entering an annual salary or a monthly one, then type the figure. The chips underneath jump to the common round numbers.
  2. Set the non-taxable allowance. A meal allowance up to 200,000 won a month is exempt from both tax and insurance, and almost every contract has one, so the default of 200,000 is right more often than zero is.
  3. Enter your household size, counting yourself. Each dependant is worth a 1.5 million won allowance against your taxable income, which is why the same salary nets more for a family than for a single filer.
  4. Enter how many children aged 8 to 20 you have. They are already counted in the household size; this second box drives the separate child tax credit.
  5. If the offer quoted thirteen months of pay as the annual figure — some do, folding the severance provision in — switch on "severance included" so the salary is treated as twelve of those thirteen.
  6. Read the breakdown. Every line shows its rate, its monthly amount and its yearly total, and the table below puts your salary in the middle of the neighbouring bands.

lightbulb Tips & gotchas

  • The employer pays the same insurance amounts again on top of yours. The cost of employing you is roughly your gross plus another ten per cent, which is worth knowing when you negotiate.
  • National pension stops counting income above a ceiling, so past roughly 6.37 million won a month that line stops growing. It is the reason take-home rises faster than gross at high salaries.
  • The neighbouring-salary table answers the question people actually have. A five million won raise does not add five million to your take-home; the table shows exactly how much of it survives.
  • Long-term care insurance is charged on your health premium, not on your pay, which is why its rate looks large and its amount looks small.
  • Everything you type stays in this browser. Nothing about your pay is sent anywhere, which is not something you can say about most salary sites.

help_outline Frequently asked questions

Why does this differ from the figure on my payslip?

Because they answer different questions. Your employer withholds tax each month from a simplified table published by the tax office, which is a deliberately rough estimate. This calculator runs the actual year-end settlement arithmetic: earned-income deduction, personal allowances, the insurance you paid, the progressive brackets, then the earned-income and child credits. The two are close but not identical, and the difference is exactly what your February settlement refunds or collects. If you want to know what the year really costs you, this number is the better one.

It says my tax is lower than I expected. Why?

The earned-income tax credit is doing the work. After the brackets produce a figure, up to 740,000 won of it is credited straight back, tapering as pay rises. Below about 30 million won a year that credit wipes out most of the liability, which is why a mid-range salary pays far less tax than the headline 15% bracket suggests.

What is not included?

Only the standard allowances are applied. Anything you have to claim yourself is not: housing loan interest, pension savings contributions, medical and education expenses, credit-card spending above the threshold, monthly rent, and donations. Every one of them reduces your tax further, so treat the result as the ceiling on what you will pay rather than the exact amount.

Are the rates current?

The rates in use are shown under the result with the period they apply to. All of them are revised annually — the pension ceiling moves every July, the health rate and the care rate are set each year, and the tax brackets change with the budget. A calculator that does not tell you which year it is answering for cannot be checked, so this one says so on the page.

Does "severance included" change the tax?

It changes the salary, and the tax follows. Severance pay is taxed separately and far more lightly than salary, so a contract quoting thirteen months as one annual number is not offering you thirteen months of taxable salary. Switching it on treats twelve of those months as pay, which is what your monthly payslip will show.
