Background Remover

Background Remover
Take the background out of a picture and save it as a transparent PNG. Everything happens on a canvas in your browser — no upload, no account, no watermark.
add_photo_alternate
Drop pictures here, paste with Ctrl+V, or click to choose. Several at once is fine.

Drop in a picture and the background comes off. It works out from the picture itself whether it is looking at a plain backdrop or a studio screen and takes the right one out, so there is no mode to choose. What it removes becomes transparent, which means what you save is a PNG you can place on any colour.

list_alt How to use Background Remover

  1. Drop a picture in, paste one with Ctrl+V, or click to choose. Several at once is fine.
  2. The background comes off straight away. The line above the picture says what it read the background as.
  3. If a piece of background survived, click it. If something you wanted was taken, turn on Touch up and paint it back.
  4. Fix the edge if you need to: shrink by a pixel to lose a pale outline, feather to soften it, and clean edge colour to take the old background back out of half-transparent pixels.
  5. Choose what goes behind — nothing, a solid colour, or the original photo blurred.
  6. Save as PNG for transparency, WebP for a smaller file that still has it, or JPEG once you have put a colour behind it.

lightbulb Tips & gotchas

  • Plain backdrops, product shots on white, screenshots, logos and green screens are what this does best. A person photographed against a busy room is the hard case, and the brush is there for it.
  • A pale rim around the cutout means the last row of old background is still attached. Shrink by one pixel and it goes.
  • Tolerance is how far from the background colour still counts as background. Raise it if too much survives; lower it if the subject is being eaten.
  • Editing happens on a copy no larger than 1600 pixels so the sliders stay instant, but the file is always saved at the original size.
  • JPEG cannot hold transparency. Put a colour behind the cutout first, or the transparent part comes out black.

help_outline Frequently asked questions

Are my pictures uploaded anywhere?

No. Everything happens on a canvas inside your browser. There is no server, no account, no upload and no usage limit — open your browser network panel while you use it and you will see no request go out. That also means a photo you would not want to hand to a stranger is safe here.

How does it decide what the background is?

Every pixel around the edge of the frame is used as a starting point, because a photograph of a thing almost always has the background touching all four sides and the subject touching none of them. If that edge colour turns out to be a strongly saturated green or blue it is treated as a studio screen and removed by hue alone, which is what lets the shadows falling on the screen go with it. Otherwise it is filled inwards by colour and stops at anything that is not background, so a white shirt in the middle of a photo taken against a white wall survives.

Is there an AI model behind this?

No, and that is deliberate. A segmentation model is tens to hundreds of megabytes that every visitor would have to download, and the licences on the good ones restrict commercial use. For plain backdrops, product shots, screenshots and green screens, matching on colour is more accurate than a model anyway, because there is nothing to guess: the background is that colour. For a person against a complicated background it is not, and the brush is the honest answer to that rather than a promise the tool cannot keep.

Why does the outline look rough on a dark background?

Edge pixels are a mixture of the subject and the old background, so a cutout from a white backdrop carries white in its outline and shows it the moment you place it on something dark. Clean edge colour undoes that mixture, and shrinking by a pixel removes the worst of it. Both are on by default.

Can I do several at once?

Yes. Drop several pictures in, work through them using the thumbnails, and Save all as ZIP writes every one at its original size with the settings you have chosen.
